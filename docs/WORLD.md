# The world

Petalfell is a continent people left slowly. It was not destroyed in one event;
roads, shrines, gates, civic works and settlements outlived the communities that
maintained them. A few people remain, mostly in the cold north. Everywhere else,
the landscape is beautiful, quiet and enormous.

Wandering is the game. A place is its own reward.

## Spatial principles

- The world is one connected authored geography, not a set of circular levels.
- Provinces describe long landscape transitions, not visible borders.
- Major rivers, mountain fronts, basins, coasts and wetlands explain travel.
- Roads and sites follow terrain and history; they do not sit on top of random
  wilderness.
- Sites are sparse enough that distance and approach matter.
- Ruins tell stories through scale, route, wear, collapse and reclamation rather
  than exposition.

## Broad regions

The accepted atlas establishes several landscape families:

- high northern snow and mountain country with the last holdouts;
- western highland/scarp country and exposed monumental stone;
- central green basins, woods, rivers and the enclosed lake system;
- eastern blossom country with long meadow transitions;
- southern wetlands, drowned delta, shallows and island approaches;
- open coasts and offshore remnants that make the continent feel larger than
  the traversed land.

Names and exact allocations live in topology data. This document owns their
narrative relationship, not coordinates.

## History visible in geography

Water and elevation shaped settlement first. Large civic works occupy crossings,
passes, defended shelves and ritual overlooks. As maintenance declined, outer
roads and exposed high sites failed first; fertile/coastal routes were held
longer. Reclamation follows moisture, shelter and broken stone joints.

This history should be inferable from approach:

- a gate narrows a real route;
- a bridge answers a real cut;
- stairs connect meaningful levels;
- a monument commands a view or crossing;
- rubble collects below an actual failure;
- vegetation density changes where people once cleared or tended ground.

## Site allocation

Significant content is selected and placed by authored topology. For each site,
record:

- permanent coordinate and domain;
- reference image/plan;
- connection to the route graph;
- terrain/hydrology reason for the location;
- narrative function and state of abandonment;
- production status.

Procedural systems may dress wilderness or realize measured damage. They may not
invent a significant place or connection.

## Tone

The visual world stays pastel, high-key and readable, with long-lens isometric
composition, soft atmosphere, restrained dark outlines and translucent water.
Scale comes from long approaches, connected terrain masses and player-relative
architecture rather than empty numerical extent.

The structural references in `world-new/` own site geometry. Shadow of the
Colossus, Elden Ring and Skyrim remain scenery/scale references only.
